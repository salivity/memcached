Memcached stats items and Key Expiration Guide

The stats items command in Memcached provides aggregate, slab-level telemetry on stored items, allowing administrators to evaluate cache retention, lifecycle behavior, and eviction patterns. While the command does not return an individual list of key names approaching expiration, it supplies critical metrics—such as the age of the oldest item in a slab class and the frequency of unread expired items—that help determine whether items are surviving until their intended time-to-live (TTL) or are being evicted prematurely due to memory constraints.

Understanding the stats items Command

Memcached groups memory allocations into distinct slab classes based on item size. Executing stats items outputs statistical data for each slab class rather than global cache totals. This granularity is essential because expiration and memory pressure often affect specific data sizes disproportionately.

When evaluating key expiration dynamics, several metrics returned by stats items are directly relevant:

  • age: The age, in seconds, of the oldest item in the slab's Least Recently Used (LRU) queue.
  • expired_unfetched: The cumulative count of items that reached their expiration time and were reclaimed without ever being requested by a client.
  • evicted and evicted_nonzero: The number of valid items discarded to make space for new writes before their explicit TTL had passed.
  • crawler_reclaimed: Items reclaimed through the background LRU crawler inspecting expired data.

Evaluating Key Expiration via LRU Age

The primary indicator of how close keys are to reaching their full lifespan is the age metric. Because Memcached organizes items in an LRU queue per slab:

  1. Age vs. TTL Comparison: If your application sets a 3,600-second (1 hour) TTL on items, but the age of the oldest item in a given slab class is consistently reported around 600 seconds, items are likely being evicted 3,000 seconds before their planned expiration due to slab exhaustion.
  2. Healthy Expiration States: If the age in a slab class regularly approaches or exceeds the configured TTL values of stored keys, keys are surviving long enough to reach their expiration window naturally.

Monitoring Unfetched Expired Keys

The expired_unfetched metric helps identify keys that have already reached expiration without serving their intended purpose. A high ratio of expired_unfetched compared to total insertions indicates that:

  • Write operations are frequently caching short-lived or low-utility data.
  • Keys are remaining in memory until their TTL expires rather than being renewed, providing an indirect measure of expiration efficiency across specific data slabs.

Limitations and Granular Key Inspection

The stats items command is designed for statistical oversight, not granular key introspection. It does not display raw key strings, individual creation timestamps, or precise expiration targets.

To locate specific keys approaching expiration rather than reviewing slab health:

  • Use the LRU Crawler via the lru_crawler metadump command, which dumps metadata—including raw keys and exact expiration timestamps—with minimal locking overhead.
  • Implement client-side logging or application-level tracing to log generated TTLs at the time of cache writes.

Used correctly, stats items serves as a macro-level diagnostic tool to verify that cache memory is appropriately sized, ensuring keys remain stored long enough to reach their defined expiration targets.
